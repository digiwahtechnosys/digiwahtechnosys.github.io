1Objective

Today, everything that happens after a travel requisition is approved — estimates, ticket options, bookings, invoices — lives in email. The accounts team has no single place to see what was approved, what it finally cost, and whether the invoice matches.

This system automates the chain from approved requisition to verified bill, and makes the Purchase Order in Zoho Books the single source of truth for the accounts team. The accountant opens one PO and sees: what was approved, for whom, at what amount, how the amount evolved, and the vendor's bill against it.

The travel agent keeps working exactly as they do today — by email — with one small addition: a simple web form for submitting estimates. No new software for them to learn, no login required.

3The process, step by step

Step 1 — Requisition and approval unchanged

An employee raises a purchase requisition in Zoho Expense. It passes through the existing L1 → L2 approval chain. Nothing changes for employees or approvers.

Step 2 — Approval triggers the system automatic

Within ~15 minutes of final approval, the automation:

  1. Creates a Draft Purchase Order in Zoho Books carrying the requisition number, traveller and trip details. No amount yet — the amount isn't known until the agent quotes.
  2. Emails the travel agent: trip details plus a secure, pre-filled form link for submitting the estimate.

From this moment the accounts team can already see the request in Zoho Books — status Draft = approved, awaiting estimate. An approval is never invisible while pricing is being worked out.

Step 3 — Agent submits the estimate form

The agent opens the form link (no login needed) and submits:

  • Estimated amounts (flight / hotel as separate lines)
  • Tentative itinerary details

The discussion of which flight and which hotel continues in email between the agent and the traveller, exactly as today. Only the agreed numbers go through the form.

Step 4 — The PO fills itself in automatic

The submission flows into the Draft PO automatically: line items, amounts, itinerary summary. The buyer reviews and confirms, and the PO is issued to the agent — by email and visible in the Vendor Portal, where the agent can formally accept it.

Step 5 — Changes are handled, not lost automatic

If the plan or price changes during booking, the agent simply resubmits the same form. The PO updates to the new amount, a revision counter increments, and Zoho Books' built-in history records who changed what and when. The accountant always sees the current agreed amount — with the full trail of how it got there.

Safety rule: once a bill has been recorded against a PO, amounts never change automatically. Any later change alerts a person and requires a deliberate correction.

Step 6 — Booking closes, invoice arrives

The agent completes the booking and submits the invoice — through the Vendor Portal's upload facility or by email. Accounts records it as a Bill linked to the PO.

Step 7 — Verification in one screen

The accountant opens the PO and sees, in one place:

  • The requisition number and who approved it
  • The current approved amount, with the complete revision history
  • The estimate submissions from the agent
  • The linked Bill from the vendor

Verification is a direct comparison — Bill vs PO — on a single screen. No searching through inboxes.

6Built-in controls

  • Nothing proceeds unapproved — the PO only ever originates from an L2-approved requisition.
  • Complete amount trail — every estimate revision is logged; the final amount is never a mystery.
  • Billed lock — amounts cannot change silently once invoicing has started.
  • Optional travel-policy check — because prices arrive after approval today, spending caps can't currently be enforced at approval time. This system can validate the agent's estimate against your travel policy (e.g., hotel caps per city or grade) before the PO advances, flagging breaches for a recorded exception decision. If a written policy with numeric caps exists, we recommend enabling this.
